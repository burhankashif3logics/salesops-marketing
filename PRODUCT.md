# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Sales leaders, founders, agency owners, and business development teams who need a clearer way to run revenue work across scattered channels. They arrive from a marketing page while evaluating whether SalesOps can replace spreadsheets, inbox handoffs, marketplace tabs, and disconnected CRM notes with one operating layer for prospecting, follow-up, and pipeline visibility.

## Product Purpose

SalesOps is an AI-assisted sales operations workspace for service businesses and growth teams. It centralizes prospect discovery, outreach, lead management, deal tracking, tasks, meetings, and performance reporting so teams can turn scattered lead sources into an organized, measurable sales pipeline.

The marketing surface should make the product feel operationally serious, trustworthy, and immediately useful. Success means a qualified buyer understands what SalesOps controls, why it reduces sales friction, and why it belongs at the center of their team's revenue workflow.

## Brand Personality

Operational and confident. SalesOps should sound like a composed sales command center, not a hype-driven AI product. The voice is direct, precise, and practical: it names real workflows, shows how channels connect, and avoids vague productivity language.

Three-word personality: **operational, confident, precise**.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS gradients, CRM-template card grids, and hype-heavy AI copy.

Specifically avoid:

- Purple-blue gradient hero sections, glowing abstract blobs, glass panels, and decorative AI motifs.
- Identical feature-card grids with icon, heading, and paragraph repeated across the page.
- Inflated claims like "10x your sales" or "AI-powered growth engine" without operational proof.
- Dashboard mockups that look like fake metrics instead of a credible sales operating system.
- Warm beige startup pages, navy-and-gold fintech cliches, and generic CRM landing-page structure.

## Design Principles

1. **Operational proof over marketing claims.** Show real sales motions: sources, leads, follow-ups, tasks, meetings, pipeline, and revenue outcomes.
2. **Command-center clarity.** The page should feel calm under pressure, with crisp hierarchy and information density that suggests control.
3. **AppleSec restraint.** Use precise spacing, high-contrast neutrals, disciplined surfaces, and quiet confidence rather than spectacle.
4. **Connected workflow narrative.** Every section should reinforce that scattered sales activity becomes one measurable system.
5. **Trust through specificity.** Copy should name concrete workflows, statuses, and decisions instead of broad AI or CRM promises.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Baseline: WCAG 2.1 AA.

All text must meet contrast requirements, interactive elements need visible keyboard focus states, and motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. The marketing page should remain readable and credible for sales teams scanning quickly on desktop during work hours and for founders reviewing on mobile between meetings.
