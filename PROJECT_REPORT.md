# SalesOps Project Report

## Executive Summary

SalesOps is a sales operations platform for managing the full revenue workflow from prospect discovery to closed deals. The product brings multiple acquisition channels into one workspace so a sales team can find opportunities, qualify prospects, manage outreach, track follow-ups, and monitor pipeline performance without switching between disconnected spreadsheets, inboxes, job boards, and manual trackers.

The platform is designed for teams that sell services through outbound campaigns, marketplace channels, and inbound demand. It gives sales reps a single operating system for day-to-day execution, while giving managers visibility into lead flow, pipeline value, channel performance, tasks, meetings, and team activity.

For a future marketing landing page, the project can be positioned as an AI-assisted sales operations workspace for service businesses and growth teams that need to turn scattered lead sources into an organized, measurable sales pipeline.

## Product Positioning

SalesOps helps sales teams centralize prospecting, outreach, pipeline management, and performance tracking in one place.

The product is best described as:

- A sales operations dashboard for multi-channel business development.
- A CRM-style workspace focused on active lead generation and follow-up.
- A channel command center for Upwork, Fiverr, LinkedIn, cold email, and inbound activity.
- A pipeline visibility tool for sales managers and founders.
- A productivity layer for sales reps handling prospects, proposals, tasks, meetings, and follow-ups.

## Core Problem

Sales teams often generate opportunities from many different places: freelance marketplaces, LinkedIn, cold email, referrals, contact forms, and manual research. Without one shared system, important context gets fragmented.

Common problems this project solves:

- Leads are spread across different tools and hard to track.
- Sales reps lose time moving data between job boards, spreadsheets, inboxes, and CRM records.
- Managers cannot easily see which channels are producing pipeline.
- Follow-ups, tasks, meetings, and deal updates are easy to miss.
- Proposal and outreach work is difficult to standardize across the team.
- Contact data collection and verification can become slow and manual.
- Sales activity is not always connected to measurable revenue outcomes.

SalesOps addresses this by giving the team a single workspace where channel activity, leads, deals, follow-ups, meetings, and performance reporting are connected.

## Target Users

### Sales Representatives

Sales reps use SalesOps to manage daily prospecting and follow-up work. They can review new opportunities, qualify leads, update statuses, track conversations, manage tasks, prepare proposals, and keep all activity attached to the right lead or deal.

### Business Development Teams

Business development teams use the platform to operate across outbound and marketplace channels. It helps them manage campaigns, research lists, LinkedIn outreach, Upwork opportunities, Fiverr leads, and inbound inquiries from one place.

### Sales Managers

Managers use SalesOps to understand pipeline health, team workload, channel performance, deal progress, and revenue movement. The dashboard surfaces the metrics they need to decide where the team should focus.

### Founders and Agency Owners

Founders and agency owners can use the product to see whether sales activity is becoming revenue. It gives them visibility into total pipeline, closed-won value, win rate, active leads, source performance, at-risk deals, and team execution.

## Main Product Capabilities

### 1. Sales Dashboard

The dashboard gives a real-time overview of sales performance. It summarizes total pipeline value, active deals, closed-won revenue, active leads, qualified leads, proposal-stage leads, and win rate.

It also provides visual sections for:

- Pipeline by stage.
- Sales activity overview.
- Channel performance.
- Team leaderboard.
- Top deals.
- Recent tasks.
- Upcoming meetings.
- Revenue goals.
- At-risk deals.
- Sales funnel.
- Lead source ROI.

This makes the dashboard the main executive view of the sales organization.

### 2. Lead Management

SalesOps includes a structured lead workspace for managing prospects from first contact through qualification and deal creation.

Lead records can include:

- Contact name and company.
- Primary and secondary email.
- Mobile and office phone.
- Source channel and source profile.
- Stage.
- Owner.
- Estimated value.
- Budget type.
- Probability.
- Expected close date.
- Last contacted date.
- Next follow-up date.
- Tags.
- Lost reason.

Each lead can also have notes, attachments, tasks, meetings, deals, and a full activity view. This gives sales reps the context they need before contacting a prospect and gives managers a history of what happened.

### 3. Deal and Pipeline Management

Deals are tracked separately from leads so the team can manage qualified opportunities with revenue values and close timelines.

Deal records support:

- Deal name.
- Linked lead.
- Company and contact details.
- Stage.
- Owner.
- Value and currency.
- Probability.
- Expected and actual close dates.
- Contract type.
- Payment terms.
- Proposal and contract links.
- Notes.
- Lost reason.

The deal workspace supports pipeline views, deal cards, tables, and deal detail pages. It helps the sales team understand deal value, weighted value, probability, days in stage, task progress, and expected close timing.

### 4. Multi-Channel Lead Generation

SalesOps is built around multiple revenue channels. Instead of treating sales as one generic pipeline, the product gives dedicated areas for the channels the team actually uses.

Supported channel areas include:

- Upwork.
- Fiverr.
- B2B outreach.
- LinkedIn outreach.
- Cold email campaigns.
- Inbound activity.

This lets the team track channel-specific activity while still rolling qualified opportunities into the main lead and deal pipeline.

### 5. Upwork Opportunity Management

The Upwork area supports job discovery, opportunity review, proposal preparation, case studies, and proposal templates.

Key capabilities include:

- Upwork job tracking.
- Saved job searches.
- Job finder views.
- Search filters for budget, job type, experience level, client history, client location, proposal volume, and other opportunity signals.
- Candidate shortlisting and promotion into tracked jobs.
- Proposal generation workflow.
- Proposal templates.
- Case study management.
- Prompt and proposal logs for reviewing output history.

This section is especially useful for service teams that rely on Upwork as a revenue channel and need a repeatable system for finding, evaluating, and responding to the right opportunities.

### 6. Fiverr Lead Tracking

The Fiverr area gives the team a dedicated place to track leads from Fiverr. It supports adding, editing, assigning, filtering, and reviewing Fiverr lead records so that marketplace inquiries can be handled consistently and connected back to the broader sales pipeline.

### 7. B2B Outreach Workspace

The B2B outreach area manages the workflow from ideal customer profile definition to verified contact data and outreach execution.

It includes:

- ICP management.
- Search links.
- Data requests.
- Contact data views.
- LinkedIn outreach.
- Email templates.
- Campaign tracking.
- Historical imports.

This creates an organized outbound workflow:

1. Define the target customer profile.
2. Save relevant prospecting searches.
3. Request or import contact data.
4. Verify and review contacts.
5. Use templates and campaigns for outreach.
6. Track LinkedIn or email activity.
7. Convert qualified responses into leads and deals.

### 8. ICP Management

SalesOps supports detailed ideal customer profiles. An ICP can include industries, company size, geography, job titles, departments, use cases, seniority levels, pain points, technology stack, exclusion criteria, tags, notes, and active/inactive status.

This helps outbound teams stay focused on the right accounts and personas instead of building broad, low-quality lead lists.

### 9. Data Requests and Contact Verification

The platform includes a data request workflow for collecting, processing, and verifying outreach contacts. Data requests can move through statuses such as draft, assigned, uploading, verifying, ready, failed, and archived.

Tracked metrics include:

- Total rows.
- Valid rows.
- Invalid rows.
- Unknown rows.
- Reused rows.
- Verification progress.
- Verification throughput.
- Completion status.

This gives the sales team visibility into the quality and readiness of prospect data before campaigns are launched.

### 10. LinkedIn Outreach

The LinkedIn outreach area tracks relationship-building activity across saved searches and outreach lists.

Supported statuses include:

- Connection sent.
- Connected.
- Message sent.
- In conversation.
- Completed.
- No response.

This makes LinkedIn outreach easier to manage as a structured sales motion rather than a manual inbox process.

### 11. Email Campaigns and Templates

SalesOps includes campaign management for cold email and B2B outreach. The template system supports main messages and multiple follow-up steps, allowing teams to standardize campaign messaging across ICPs and outreach sequences.

This is useful for maintaining message consistency, improving handoff between team members, and connecting campaign activity to lead generation outcomes.

### 12. Inbound Activity Tracking

Inbound activity has its own workspace for tracking inquiries and responses from non-outbound sources. This helps teams separate inbound demand from outbound and marketplace channels while still measuring qualified pipeline and conversion.

### 13. Tasks and Follow-Ups

Tasks help the team stay on top of follow-ups, next steps, and internal ownership.

Tasks can be associated with leads or deals and tracked by:

- Title.
- Assignee.
- Creator.
- Status.
- Due date.
- Task type.
- Related lead or deal.

Lead and deal detail pages surface open tasks, overdue items, completed work, and upcoming activity so reps do not lose track of next steps.

### 14. Meetings

Meetings can be managed across leads and deals. The platform tracks meeting title, type, timing, status, organizer, related lead, related deal, and calendar details.

Meeting views help the team see upcoming calls, completed meetings, average duration, and meeting history connected to each opportunity.

### 15. Notes, Attachments, and Activity History

SalesOps supports notes, file attachments, and activity tracking around sales records. This is important for handoffs, manager review, proposal context, and keeping a complete history of each prospect or deal.

Lead detail pages include sections for:

- Overview.
- Notes.
- Attachments.
- Tasks.
- Meetings.
- Deals.
- Activity.

This keeps the full customer conversation in one place.

### 16. Team and Admin Management

The platform includes user management and role-based access for protected areas such as settings and admin users. Managers and admins can manage configuration, team access, and operating settings without mixing those controls into the main sales workflow.

## End-to-End Workflow

A typical sales workflow in SalesOps looks like this:

1. The team defines ICPs for the markets they want to target.
2. Reps or researchers create prospecting searches and data requests.
3. Contact data is uploaded, checked, verified, and organized.
4. Outreach starts through LinkedIn, cold email, Upwork, Fiverr, or inbound handling.
5. Promising conversations are converted into leads.
6. Qualified leads become deals.
7. Tasks, meetings, notes, and attachments are tracked against each opportunity.
8. Managers monitor pipeline, channel performance, team activity, and at-risk deals.
9. The team uses performance data to improve targeting, follow-ups, campaigns, and proposal quality.

Managers can compare performance across Upwork, Fiverr, LinkedIn, cold email, and inbound channels. This helps the team understand which channels are producing activity, pipeline, and revenue.

### Faster Follow-Up

Tasks, meetings, last-contact data, next-follow-up dates, and activity views make it easier for reps to stay organized and respond on time.

### More Consistent Outreach

Templates, ICPs, campaign records, case studies, and proposal workflows help standardize how the team communicates with prospects.

### Stronger Pipeline Control

Pipeline value, deal stages, weighted value, probability, close dates, at-risk deals, and revenue goals give managers a clearer view of revenue health.

### Cleaner Outbound Data

The data request and verification workflow helps the team work with cleaner contact data before launching campaigns.

## Differentiators

SalesOps is differentiated by its focus on service-sales execution across multiple acquisition channels.

Unlike a generic CRM, the platform includes dedicated workflows for:

- Marketplace sales through Upwork and Fiverr.
- B2B outbound through ICPs, search links, data requests, LinkedIn, and cold email.
- Inbound activity tracking.
- Proposal and case study workflows.
- Channel-level performance reporting.
- Lead-to-deal conversion with tasks, meetings, notes, and attachments.

This makes it more specific to the operating reality of agencies, consulting companies, software service providers, and outbound-heavy business development teams.
